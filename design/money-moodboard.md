# Ollie Money Module — Moodboard (v2)

v1's reference set was web-design-blog-recommended and ended up flattening the page into Aesop-cosplay. v2's references are pulled from a designer's actual studio shelf — print first, vintage poster school second, contemporary craft-product third, one wild card. Every entry names a specific issue / spread / artifact, what to borrow, what NOT to borrow.

The v1 reference list is **explicitly retired** — none of those 8 references appears in v2.

## Print references (3 — from a designer's studio shelf)

| # | Reference | Type | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Pirelli Calendar 1972, Hans Feurer issue — January spread masthead** | print, large-format calendar | A 240pt serif "1972" sits column-left, a single small ink panel column-right contains the Pirelli mark. Two filled surfaces, the rest is paper. Borrow: the *exactly two ink surfaces* contract — money's masthead + patterns anchor + textured hero band, nothing else filled. NOT: the photography column or the corporate red. |
| 2 | **Aperture Magazine #243 (Summer 2021, "Native America" issue) — Cara Romero photo-essay opener** | print quarterly | The opener spread sets a 36pt italic-Roman headline column-left while a 14pt body block sits at column-RIGHT with a hard 56px indent. The asymmetry is the page's hand. Borrow: the "noticed" section's column-right indent treatment, exact metric. Also: the small `¶` glyph sitting outside the text column as a marginalia marker. NOT: the photo opener — Ollie carries no images. |
| 3 | **MoMA "Items: Is Fashion Modern?" exhibition catalog (2017), Section 4 binding** | hardcover exhibition catalog | The catalog is printed on uncoated cream stock where the paper grain is visible at reading distance. Section dividers are 32×32mm solid ink rectangles holding a single white sans numeral. Two textures across the whole book: cream stock and solid ink. Borrow: this is the *exact reference* for v2's ink-anchor-block treatment. The sage-numeral-on-ink mirrors the catalog's white-numeral-on-black. NOT: the section colors used elsewhere in the catalog. |

## Vintage references (2 — poster-school discipline)

| # | Reference | Type | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| 4 | **Tadeusz Trepkowski — "1944 Nie" (1953, Polish poster school)** | Polish anti-war poster | A vast pale ground with one small dark mass — a falling bomb silhouette — and one italic word. The poster's force comes from 95% void anchored by 5% true ink. Borrow: this *is* the v2 thesis. The 5% rule. The single ink mass against the cream field. The italic word as the only typographic flourish. NOT: the bomb image or the political subject — borrow the *visual logic*, not the iconography. |
| 5 | **Tanaka Ikko — Nihon Buyō poster (1981, Japanese poster school)** | silkscreen poster | A geisha face reduced to flat ink shapes against a cream ground, with one small red rectangle anchoring the bottom-right corner. The composition holds because the small color anchor balances the large pale negative space. Borrow: the principle of a small high-contrast element earning the rest of the composition's calm. Maps to v2's ‡ printer's mark in `--umber` sitting in the hero band's bottom-right corner. NOT: the figurative drawing or the red — Ollie's accent is `--umber`, used at glyph scale. |

## Wild card (1 — the thing you'd keep)

| # | Reference | Type | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| 8 | **Mast Brothers chocolate — Madagascar bar wrapper (the navy + cream tessellated pattern, 2014–2018 era)** | physical product packaging | The wrapper is uncoated cream stock printed with a hand-set wood-type product line, a dotted printer's rule, and a small umber typographic ornament (a `‡` double-dagger) in the lower-right corner. ## Voice anchors (unchanged — voice was never the v1 problem)

- **Aperture editorial captions** — "the photograph was made in 1971 in Mojave, California." Factual, unhurried, no marketing. Tone we ship in `finance.signal.*`.
- **Drake's London product copy** — "this lambswool will pill. that's wool." Conversational, no marketing voice. Tone for empty states.
- **VOID's own pod-b voice library** (`design-pitches/voice-library.html`). Banned-phrase list is canonical.

## Anti-references — what we explicitly are not doing

- **Mint / Rocket Money** — every shame trigger. "You overspent in groceries by 18%!" red callouts, pie charts that brag, percentage-of-income comparisons. Same anti-reference v1 named. Still the floor.
- **YNAB classic** — over-categorization. Ollie's transactions get one free-text category and that's it.
- **Stripe Dashboard** — data-density-as-virtue. No "+12.4% MoM" green/red badges. No grids of KPI tiles.
- **Apple Wallet** — gradient card stack. Ollie isn't a wallet, isn't a card metaphor.
- **Aesop's website (the v1 trap)** — beige-on-beige with a single serif sentence floating in negative space. v1 ended up here by accident. v2 names it as the thing to avoid. Aesop is calm-but-dead; v2 wants calm-with-tension. The difference is the ink-anchor + texture.
- **Linear / Hinge** — explicitly retired as v2 references. They are too obvious as moodboard items for an editorial fintech redesign in 2026. Their patterns are absorbed at this point; pointing at them again is a tell.

## Palette anchors (v2)

- **Mast Brothers wrapper cream** — uncoated, slightly warm. `--bone #FAFAF7` + `--paper-grain` overlay = this exact reading.
- **Polish poster school ink** — never-quite-black, with paper grain visible through the print. `--ink #14140F` printed onto `--bone` reads this way when texture is present.
- **Tanaka Ikko's small color anchor** — single high-contrast accent at glyph scale. Maps to `--umber` `‡` printer's mark in the hero, used once.
- **Rapha 100 sage trim** — small italic-color moment per spread. Maps to `--accent #2E5D43` used in exactly three italic moments per page.

## Typography anchors (v2)

- **EB Garamond (free Google Fonts substitute for PP Editorial Old / Lyon Text)** — transitional serif with a true italic. Has `tnum` and `lnum`. Carries the hero numeral and all display headings.
- **Cormorant Garamond Italic** — pairs with EB Garamond Roman; gives the italic moments a writer's hand rather than a synthetic slant.
- **Inter Tight (free substitute for Söhne)** — humanist sans with more personality than Inter standard. Body + amount-list. Tabular figures via `font-variant-numeric`.
- **JetBrains Mono (free substitute for Söhne Mono)** — visible drawing on the digits and the slashes. Labels + meta + the `§` and `¶` marginalia glyphs.

## Motion anchors (v2)

- **Polish-poster letterpress feel** — there is no motion in the references. v2 borrows the *stillness* of the texture: paper grain does not animate. Everything else can; the substrate doesn't.
- **Mast Brothers wrapper "draw-on" of the dotted rule** — when a designer hand-rules a printer's mark on paper, it draws once and stays. v2's hand-rule under the masthead draws on once at mount, then is static forever.
- **Tanaka Ikko's stillness** — silkscreen posters do not breathe. The hero numeral's 6s breathe survives only because it's the one *living* moment on the page; everything else holds.
